# ADR-012: One role ladder, two pure functions, a doorless pass-through — workspace-membership RBAC

> **Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-01 (*accepted as written*) / **2026-10-03** (*finding (a) — the scoped list — **closed**, py/ts parity restored*) · **Deciders:** I (learner, Day 10-11)
> **Supersedes:** nothing — it *rides on* ADR-011 (badge reader) and ADR-008 (doorless cellar)
> **Superseded by:** — *(the moment a **payer** exists: Day 12-13 Razorpay is where the two roles become two **products**, see "When this is revisited")*

## Context

ADR-011 (day 8-9) built **who-you-are**: a thin, doorless **badge reader** that *verifies* a Bearer JWT and leaves a `request.user` claim on the request. Its closing seam said it *deliberately does not build authorization* — **"'is this user an owner of this workspace' is Day 10-11."** This ADR is that hand-off.

The context I could not get out of my head: **this is no longer a single-user toy.** The moment a real team uses Taskflow — where I am the owner and *someone else is a member* — three *differently-dangerous* things must be decided, **before** I let a second human write to my data:

1. **Who can do *what*?** (a member may *create* a task in my workspace; may a member *delete the workspace*? may they *invite others*?)
2. **Whose *things* are *theirs*?** (a badged member of *workspace A* asks `GET /projects` — must they see *workspace B*'s tasks? The prompt's route-table said **no**.)
3. **How do I *get* a workspace to a teammate?** (an *invite* by user-id — with what state, and what happens if they're already a member?)

And the standing **foundry rules** that bound the shape of *any* answer: **ADR-010** (CI is **zero-service** — no tenant, no key), the **ADR-001 parity wall** (the *same* decision on `:8000` py and `:8001` ts; every *named* asymmetry is an ADR), and **ADR-006** (drain, not recreate) / **ADR-008** (doorless cellar — tests talk to fake cellars).

## Decision

**I built one role ladder, two pure functions, a doorless pass-through, and membership-scoped list routes — *identically in both kitchens* — and named the three places the parity wall *should* hold but *did not* survive.**

- **A role ladder of exactly two.** `role` is `member | owner`, and rank is a *number* (`member < owner`), in `constants.ts` / `constants.py`. *"Do you hold the right badge for what you just tried?"* is a *single comparison*: `rank(have) >= rank(need)`. (This is the "role ladder as data" pattern — a rank function over an ordered role set, the shape every real authorizer reduces to.)
- **Two pure decision functions** — `assertRole(role, route)` (TS) / `assert_role(...)` (PY) — that return a pass or one of **two exact 403 strings** (`Not a member of this workspace`, `Requires the owner role`). Purity is the *point*: the decision is a function of `(sub, route)`, so it is **unit-testable offline** — *no tenant, no key, no DB, no browser* (ADR-010).
- **A doorless pass-through.** The authorizer *reads* the badge the reader (ADR-011) left on the request and **never re-verifies it**; **when there is no `Auth0Config`, every call passes through as a doorless guest.** This is what let the *whole* RBAC layer land in both kitchens with **zero new CI dependencies** — the ADR-010 rule, *applied a second time*.
- **Gating vs scoping are *different answers*.** *Writes/delete/invite* are **gated** by the role ladder (the `minRoleFor` map: PATCH/DELETE workspace = owner, PATCH/DELETE project & issue = member, POST …/members = owner). But a **list** route (`GET /workspaces|/projects|/issues`) is *not* gated — it is **scoped**: the kitchen returns *only the caller's* rows via a join through `workspace_members` on the badge (`findWorkspacesFor(sub)` / `find_workspaces_for(sub)`). A stranger with a *valid* badge gets `[]`, **not everyone else's data.**
- **A `workspace_members` table** (one role row per user per workspace; `uq_ws_member` so an *invite* upserts or `409`s rather than double-issuing). **Create** mints the creator as **owner** in *one transaction* (`save_workspace_with_owner`), so no workspace is ever briefly ownerless.
- **Named the three *asymmetries*** the audit found in the working tree (these are the *consequences*, and the *work* for day 12-13):
  - **(a)** The **"mine" scope** held on *workspaces* (both kitchens) and *projects/issues in PY* — but the **TS `GET /projects` / `GET /issues`** list routes still call `findAll*` = *everyone's*. A real cross-workspace info-leak *on the TS track only* — the *one* true RBAC gap. **[the parity wall *broke* here; this ADR is what names it]**
  - **(b)** PY routes carry **commented-out** membership checks; TS routes carry **active** inline `findMembership` checks *on top of* the middleware. The two kitchens are *not* "the same shape" in *how* they enforce — only in the *pure function*. *Recommendation:* converge on **middleware-only** (delete the redundant inline *and* the commented blocks) so the *enforcement* shape, not just the decision, matches.
  - **(c)** The **`workspace_members` migration** was added as *live schema* in both `db.ts` / `models.py` but **no named migration cell** was appended — the tables still come from `create_all` / `db:push`, *ADR-008's "first-time-renovator" debt*. (Natural companion to the day-11 migration work; zero-service.)

## Why (the *why* — what I actually learned)

- **An authorizer is a *verifier of role*, not a *verifier of token*.** The day's *core* lesson, and the direct payoff of ADR-011's split: the **badge reader** answers *"are you who you claim to be?"* (authentication); the **authorizer** answers *"may this badge do this?"* (authorization) and *trusts* the reader — it reads `request.user.sub`, it never re-checks a JWT. *Splitting the two is why the authorizer is a **pure function** I can unit-test at all — no Auth0 tenant, no network.* (Auth0's own model: *we* treat the badge as *read-only*; the `sub` **claim** is the only thing I reason about.)
- **The *ladder* is data, so *grow the roles* by editing one line.** Because rank is a number over `member | owner`, a future **`editor`** slot between them is *a change to the ladder*, not a rewrite of every route. *This is the shape that makes the "day-12-13 the payer exists" story *possible without a refactor*: I'll *promote a member to editor/manager* when a workspace *pays* — the ladder has room.*
- **The *list* route's security is *membership*, and the *where clause is the feature*.** The quiet load-bearing idea: a badge tells you *who*; it says nothing about *whose things they may see*. So the *list* routes **scope** (join on the badge) rather than gate, and *a stranger with a valid badge gets an empty list, not your data.* (The classic multi-tenant where-clause-carrying-the-tenant — the *absence* of the join is the bug, *exactly* finding (a).)
- **A *second* uncovered-403 is born, and I *name* it instead of hiding it** — the clean twin of ADR-011's *uncovered-badged 403*. The *role* 403s (non-member / wrong-role) fire **only when a real badge is present**, which **needs an Auth0 tenant** → **forbidden by ADR-010** → *they cannot fire in CI*. So the day closes with 403 *semantics I cannot exercise in CI*, and the honest move is to **name** the gap and **resolve it the moment a badged e2e is permitted (Day 12+)**. *(What *is* covered offline: the **pure decision** — the two messages, the doorless pass-through, the rank. That is the split.)*
- **A *two-role* ladder is *deliberately* a *cut*, and I *state the cut* rather than pretend it's a *lifecycle*.** The real-world reference I kept in mind — **Plane** — models a member with a *role* **and** an *invitation state* (`pending` → `active` → *revoked*), because a member *joins via an invite they must accept*. **I built role-only: an invite is *immediate* (upsert-or-`409`), with no `pending` / accept / revoke lifecycle.** *The cut earns its keep for a two-person team (there is no one to "accept" — a dev invites a dev); the **lifecycle** comes *exactly* when a *payer* is invited to a **paid** workspace, i.e. **Day 12-13**. I state it as an ADR, so day 12-13 *reads* that state and builds it deliberately, instead of day 12-13 *accidentally* assuming "invite = active" was a decided feature.*

## Consequences

- **Positive.** The **decision** (role ladder + doorless + scoping) is a **pure function** → *fully unit-testable offline* (`test_authorize.py` / `test/authorize.test.ts`), and the *whole* layer lands CI-clean (ADR-010 held: **PY 25 · TS 38, zero services**). A *second* human can now *use* the product *safely*: they can *read their own* workspaces and *create/edit/delete* within their role — and get a clean **403** (not a 500) the moment they over-reach.
- **Negative / debt (all *named*; the *lead* ones are zero-service fixes for day 12-13).**
  - The **TS `GET /projects` / `GET /issues`** info-leak (finding **a**) — the *parity-wall break* the day named. **Closed this day:** the scoped-list is now a *both-kitchens invariant* — the pending TS diff adds `findProjectsFor` / `findIssuesFor` and re-wires the two list routes, so a badged user sees *only their workspaces' rows* (the PY twin is already committed). *The *proof* of the scoped *branch* (stranger → `[]`) is still **doorless-unexercised** in CI (it needs a real badge + a second workspace) → it **joins the *uncovered-403* set below** and resolves with the same named **e2e** — so the *fix* is done; only its *doorless proof* is deferred. **The *real* lead defect day 12-13 must first close is (b)+(c).**
  - The **two-kitchen enforcement asymmetry** (finding **b**): the same *decision* is enforced by *different shapes*, and the TS inline checks *dead-code in CI* + *double*-403 in a real door. Converging to **middleware-only** is a small, zero-service, *both-kitchens* cleanup — **a *lead* day 12-13 debt.**
  - The **missing named migration** (finding **c**): the roster is only *live schema*; the *cellar* (ADR-008) still lacks the *named* `workspace_members` cell. *(Companion to the day-11 migration work.)*
  - The **uncovered-badged 403s** (twins ADR-011's): the role 403s *cannot* be CI-exercised → *resolved the day a badged e2e is permitted (Day 12-13)*, which is *exactly* when the **real** `auth.test.ts` lands (a `docker compose` up of a *fake* Auth0 + fake DB = *local dev*, *not* the CI robot).
- **Operational.** **Doorless CI is *preserved*:** the authorizer passes through with no `Auth0Config`, so *adding RBAC added zero CI surface* — the ADR-008/010 **zero-service** rule survives *two* security layers (day 8-9 badge reader, day 10-11 *authorizer*), *both* by the same "pure function + doorless pass" move. **And** the day's *one hard dependency* (the **Auth0 tenant**, *real*, not a CI one) is the *dev-machine* thing only — *the authorizer reads the claim, never the network*.

## When this is revisited (and what *triggers* the revisit)

- **Day 12-13 — the *payer*.** The trigger is **Razorpay**: a *workspace* that **pays** is a *different product* from one that was *invited free-tier*. The **two roles** (owner / member) become **two products** — and the ladder **earns its keep** by *gaining the `editor` slot between them* (a member of a *paid* workspace who can *see everything but not invite or delete*). **That** is the revisit: *this ADR deliberately built a *two*-role ladder for a *two*-person team; it is designed to be *promoted* to a *many-role* ladder the moment money enters the picture — and ADR-012 is the record that says the *cut* was **known**, not **forgotten.***
- **The *lifecycle*.** The moment the payer is *invited* to a *paid* workspace, the role-only invite must **grow** the Plane-style `pending → active → revoked` **state** around it (see "Why" cut). **State is the *one* thing a two-person dev team doesn't need and a *real* SaaS does.**
- **What ADR will supersede / extend.** None supersedes 012 — it *rides on* 011 + 008. The **payments** ADR (ADR-013, day 12-13) *extends* it: *a workspace's **plan** (free / paid) joins the **role** ladder into **who-can-see-what** on the *list* routes. That is the *real* "Make it Right" climax: **authn (011) ⊗ authz (012) ⊗ billing (013) = a *product other humans actually use and pay for.***
